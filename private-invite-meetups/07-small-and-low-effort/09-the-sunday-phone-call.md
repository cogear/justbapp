# The Sunday phone call

*How to stop scheduling a friendship and start keeping one.*

## We Should Catch Up

There's a name in your phone you think about more often than you talk to. You know the one. Every few months you send the same message — *we should catch up soon* — and they say *yes, definitely*, and you both mean it. And then nothing happens.

It's not indifference. It's not drift in the emotional sense. You still care about this person. You still want to hear the actual sound of their voice and ask the questions that don't work over text. But the months pass, and the person who would have picked up on the first ring becomes someone you'd feel strange calling out of nowhere.

Meanwhile you follow their life from the outside. Photos. Announcements. The headlines of someone whose details you used to have.

This is one of the most common quiet losses in adult life. And it has a fix that is almost embarrassingly simple.

## Why It Doesn't Happen

A catch-up has to be arranged. Someone proposes a time. Someone checks a calendar, apologizes, proposes another. By the third message it feels like scheduling a dentist appointment. Nobody wants to be the person asking a fourth time, so the thread goes quiet, and the goodwill just sits there unused.

There's something else underneath the scheduling problem. We wait until we have news. Something worth reporting. As if a phone call needs to be justified by an event — a promotion, a move, a breakup, a trip. We treat friendship like a newsletter, and then we feel like we haven't published in too long to reach out.

But the people you're closest to didn't get that way because you had good material. You got close because you were around. Because you showed up to the ordinary parts.

## Let the Time Decide

So stop arranging. Pick a time once, and let the time do the deciding from then on. Sunday morning. Sunday evening. Whatever hour is already quiet in both of your lives.

Then it stops being a request. It becomes a standing invitation. You're hosting something that doesn't need a room or chairs or anything cleaned. You just have to show up at the same hour, week after week, and let the conversation happen or not happen.

The point isn't any particular conversation. The point is that there's a slot in the week with their name on it, and neither of you has to ask permission. Asking is what kills things. A standing time removes the ask entirely.

And it works both directions. Some weeks you'll be the one who needed it, and you won't find that out until you're already talking.

## Dial Anyway

Call even when you have nothing to report. Especially then. *Nothing much* is a complete answer, and the twenty minutes after it will fill themselves. You don't need a topic. You need a habit.

You can wash dishes. Fold laundry. Walk to the end of the street and back. A phone call is the one kind of gathering where you're allowed to be doing something else with your hands. It doesn't require your full attention or a clean house or a good mood. It just requires a phone and a person on the other end.

And it can be short. Fifteen minutes, forty Sundays a year, is more of a friendship than one ambitious dinner in June that you both keep postponing until it dies. Frequency beats ambition, every time. The small and regular thing builds something the big and rare thing cannot.

If you're usually the one who dials, let that be fine. Someone has to be the host. Keeping score of who called last is how a good thing quietly ends. Reciprocity matters, but scorekeeping kills it. Just call.

## Pick Up

Some Sundays it'll be dull. Weather, work, a story you've already heard. That's not failure. That's what a regular life sounds like from the inside. You're not trying to produce a great conversation. You're trying to stay in someone's life, and let them stay in yours.

There's a version of your week where you talk to someone you love without arranging it, without earning it, without performing. It's just a habit with a day attached.

You don't have to prepare anything. You don't have to be interesting. You just have to pick up, or dial.
