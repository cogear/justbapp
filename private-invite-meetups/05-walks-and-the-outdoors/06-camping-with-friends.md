# Camping with friends

*The camping trip in your group chat has been sitting there for two years. Here's how to actually make it happen — and why it matters.*

## The trip nobody books

Somewhere in your group chat, there's a camping trip. It's been sitting there for two years. Maybe three. Everybody wants to go. Nobody has booked anything.

That trip isn't stuck because you're all too busy. It's stuck because nobody has said the one sentence that starts it: *I'll handle it.*

That's it. That's the whole barrier. Not logistics, not schedules, not the cost of gear. Nobody has picked up the weight of being the person who decides.

This isn't really a trip, when you think about it. It's a room you build outside for one night, and then take down. Someone has to be the one who builds it. And everyone is waiting for that someone to be someone else.

## The trip that's too big to lift

Here's what happens in your head. Four nights. A mountain. Gear that costs more than a flight. A destination worthy of the planning. The trip grows until it has a soundtrack.

So you research. Tabs open. Sleeping pads compared. Stove systems weighed against each other. And the plan gets heavier until nobody can lift it, and another summer passes with the tent still in its bag in the closet.

The strange thing is, the epic version isn't even what you're after. You don't miss the mountain. You miss your friends — for longer than three hours, in a place where nobody has to leave for anything.

## One night, close by

Here's the version that actually happens. One night. A campground forty minutes from town. A site with a picnic table and a water tap you can walk to. Cars in sight. That's allowed. That's fine.

It won't be the trip you imagined. It'll be the trip you took.

And nobody comes home talking about the view. They talk about the thing that went wrong — the stove that wouldn't light, the tarp that collapsed, the raccoon — and the two hours around the fire when nobody was in a hurry. The thing that went wrong is the story. The fire is the memory.

## Hosting outdoors is mostly errands

Hosting out here is mostly errands. You book the site. You bring the box — a pot, matches, salt, bin bags, the spare headlamp nobody remembered to pack. That's most of the job. Showing up prepared.

Then you tell people exactly what to bring. Not *bring whatever.* A sleeping bag, a warm layer, and one job each. You're on breakfast. You're on firewood. You're on coffee.

Those jobs aren't work you're offloading. They're the whole reason it works. Somebody needs you to show up with the eggs. That's a good feeling — being needed for something small and concrete. It gives people a way to belong to the trip that isn't just *thanks for inviting me.*

Cook something dumb. Foil packets. Instant noodles at eleven at night. The food isn't the point. It's a reason to stand close together with your hands busy, looking at the same fire instead of each other. There's a looseness in that. Conversation moves differently when everyone's hands are doing something.

Then it gets dark, and the fire becomes the room. Nobody's phone works well. Nobody leaves early, because there's nowhere to go. Conversations get long in a way they almost never do at home, where someone is always one glance from checking the time.

## Morning, soft-faced

You'll sleep badly. Someone will be cold. Someone will wake up with a stone-shaped bruise on their hip. That's part of it — you agreed to be a little uncomfortable in the same place, and that does something a restaurant can't. It makes the comfort you give each other — the extra blanket passed over, the coffee made strong — actually mean something.

Then morning. Wet grass, bad coffee, everyone quiet and soft-faced, nobody performing yet. Somebody hands you a mug without asking if you want one. That's the part you'll want back next year.

## Just pick the weekend

You don't have to be an outdoors person. You need a date, a site, and a box in your boot. Pick the weekend before you feel ready. Then tell them you've booked it. Not *we should go sometime.* Tell them where, when, and what to bring.

The trip in the group chat has been waiting for two years. It's not waiting for better gear or a better destination. It's waiting for someone to say *I booked a site for the 14th, bring a sleeping bag and six eggs.* Be that person.
