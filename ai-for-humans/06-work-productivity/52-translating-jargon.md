# Translating Jargon

## Jargon: Useful Tool, Accidental Wall

Every profession has its own shorthand.

Medicine leans on Latin. Law has procedures and clauses. Technology loves acronyms. And in many ways, that specialized vocabulary is a feature, not a flaw. When the stakes are high—a diagnosis, a contract, a system failure—precision matters.

But the same language that creates precision can also create distance. It can turn a conversation into a performance where one person speaks and the other person nods, hoping the important parts will somehow sink in later.

And if you’ve ever left an appointment, a meeting, or a phone call thinking, *I didn’t understand most of that, but I didn’t want to look foolish*, you’re not alone.

## Maria and the Business Words She “Should” Know

Maria owns a bakery that’s grown from a farmers market stall to two storefronts. She’s good at what she does: planning menus, managing staff, handling customers, and making a product people love.

Then her accountant says: “Your EBITDA looks healthy, but your current ratio is concerning. You should look at your inventory turnover.”

Maria nods. She writes it down. She smiles politely. This is what competent business owners do… right?

But driving home, it hits her: she understood almost none of it.

And here’s the painful part: those words clearly matter. They were said with a tone that suggested urgency. Maria doesn’t want to ignore them. She just doesn’t have a translation key.

That evening she asks an AI: “I’m a bakery owner. Can you explain these terms in plain English, using examples from my kind of business?”

Suddenly the fog lifts.

- **EBITDA** becomes: *How much profit your bakery makes from selling baked goods, before loans or taxes.*
- **Current ratio** becomes: *Money coming in soon versus what you owe soon—if it’s low, you might scramble to pay suppliers before customer payments arrive.*
- **Inventory turnover** becomes: *How quickly you use up flour and butter. High turnover is good—nothing goes stale.*

Now Maria doesn’t just “know the definitions.” She understands what’s happening in her business. Good sales can still coexist with a cash crunch. Over-ordering ingredients can quietly bleed money through waste, storage issues, and tied-up cash.

Translation turns vague worry into something she can act on.

## This Isn’t Just Finance—It’s Everywhere

Business jargon is only one flavor of this problem. The real issue is bigger: modern life requires us to interact with experts constantly.

### Health: When the words feel like a different planet  
James is caring for his elderly mother when a doctor mentions “renal function decline.” He leaves with lab results and numbers that might as well be written in code.

At home he asks an AI to explain what the terms mean, what the values generally indicate, and what questions to bring to the follow-up. He’s not trying to play doctor—he’s trying to be an informed advocate for someone he loves.

### Creative work: When feedback isn’t actually clear  
Priya is a designer. A client tells her the design needs better “messaging hierarchy” and more “category vernacular.”

Priya is talented, but she’s not fluent in marketing-speak. After she gets those phrases decoded into plain language—what to emphasize, what words the industry expects, how to guide the eye—she can revise the work in a way that actually addresses the client’s concerns.

The problem wasn’t her skill. The problem was translation.

## What AI Is Really Doing Here: Lowering the Cost of Understanding

It’s worth saying clearly: translation is not expertise.

Understanding EBITDA doesn’t make Maria an accountant. Knowing what “renal function” means doesn’t make James a physician. But the goal isn’t to replace experts—it’s to engage with them meaningfully.

When you can translate jargon into plain language, you can:

- Ask better questions  
- Spot gaps or assumptions  
- Make decisions with more confidence  
- Catch misunderstandings early  
- Feel less intimidated (which matters more than we admit)

Experts often forget what it’s like not to know their field. They’ve internalized the vocabulary so deeply that they don’t realize they’re speaking a dialect.

AI can act like a patient, nonjudgmental interpreter—one you can pause, rewind, and question without feeling embarrassed.

## How to Use AI as a “Jargon Translator” (Without Getting Misled)

A few simple prompts can dramatically improve your results:

- “Explain this like I’m new to the topic, using a bakery/parenting/freelance example.”
- “What does this term mean, and why would someone mention it?”
- “What are common misunderstandings about this?”
- “What questions should I ask my accountant/doctor/lawyer next?”
- “Give me a quick summary in 3 bullets.”

And one important habit: treat the output as a draft understanding, not the final truth. If the topic is medical, legal, or financial, use AI to prepare for the expert conversation—not to replace it.

## When the Wall Becomes a Bridge

Maria goes back to her accountant and asks better questions. Not confrontational ones—curious ones. Practical ones. The accountant is pleasantly surprised because many clients don’t do that. They nod and hope.

That’s the real shift: the jargon that once separated them now becomes a bridge. Maria can participate in decisions that affect her business instead of feeling like she’s watching from the outside.

## Closing Thought

We all have areas where we’re fluent and areas where we’re lost. The goal isn’t to become experts in everything—it’s to understand enough to advocate for yourself, collaborate well, and make informed choices. Sometimes, all it takes is a good translator.
