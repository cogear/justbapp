# Explain It Like I’m Five

We’ve all had that moment: someone explains something “simple,” and suddenly you’re drowning in acronyms, buzzwords, and sentences that feel like they were written for a robot—by a robot.

In a world where AI is becoming part of everyday life, the real superpower isn’t knowing the fanciest terms. It’s being able to translate big, complex ideas into something a real human can understand. Especially when that human is tired, busy, or just trying to get through the day.

This is the heart of “Explain It Like I’m Five”: not talking down to people, but making ideas feel friendly and usable.

## The Wall of Jargon

Jargon is like a wall built out of “expert language.” On one side are people who already know the terms. On the other side are everyone else—smart, curious people who simply haven’t been invited into the conversation in a clear way.

In AI, this wall shows up fast. Words like *models*, *tokens*, *neural networks*, *training data*, *hallucinations*, *fine-tuning*, and *embeddings* start flying around like everyone should automatically know what they mean.

And the problem isn’t that these words exist. Specialized language can be helpful among specialists. The problem is when jargon becomes a substitute for clarity—or worse, a way to sound impressive while leaving people confused.

Confusion doesn’t just slow learning. It creates shame. People start thinking, “Maybe I’m not technical enough,” when the truth is often, “This explanation wasn’t human enough.”

## The Magic of Translation

Translation is not dumbing things down. It’s pulling the meaning out of complexity and setting it on the table in plain sight.

A good translation does two things:

1. **It preserves the core idea.**  
2. **It changes the packaging so the listener can actually use it.**

For example, instead of saying: “The model hallucinates when it generates outputs not grounded in the input distribution,” you could say: “Sometimes AI will confidently make things up, like a kid telling a story. It sounds sure, but it might not be true.”

That’s the magic. Suddenly, you know what to watch for. You know why checking matters. You can work with the tool instead of feeling intimidated by it.

This kind of translation isn’t only for beginners. Even experts benefit from returning to simple language because it reveals whether an idea is truly understood—or just memorized.

## Meeting People Where They Are

The best explanations don’t start with what *you* know. They start with what the other person already understands.

If you’re talking to a friend who loves cooking, AI might make more sense as a “recipe-following helper” that learns patterns from examples. If you’re talking to someone in wellness, you might compare it to a “reflection tool” that can generate prompts and options—but still needs human judgment to choose what fits.

Meeting people where they are also means respecting their emotional state. When someone is stressed, their brain has less room for complexity. When someone feels judged, they stop asking questions.

A helpful explanation has a tone to it: patient, curious, and collaborative. It quietly says, “You belong here. Let’s figure this out together.”

And if you work in wellness or personal growth, this should sound familiar. Real growth happens when people feel safe enough to learn.

## Beyond One-Size-Fits-All

Not everyone needs the same explanation.

Some people want the quick version—just enough to make a decision. Others want a deeper understanding because it helps them feel grounded and confident. Some want examples. Some want steps. Some want the “why” more than the “how.”

One-size-fits-all explanations fail because they assume there’s one correct level of detail. But the best communicators adjust like a dimmer switch:

- **Kid-level:** “AI is a tool that guesses what comes next based on what it has seen before.”  
- **Everyday-level:** “AI looks for patterns in lots of examples, then uses those patterns to generate answers or suggestions.”  
- **Practical-level:** “AI can help brainstorm, summarize, and draft, but it can be wrong—so treat it like a fast assistant, not a final authority.”

Flexibility is the point. You’re not just delivering information. You’re building a bridge.

## Building Understanding

When you translate well, you do more than share knowledge—you create confidence.

Understanding isn’t built by dumping information. It’s built through:

- **Simple language**
- **Concrete examples**
- **Honest limits** (what the tool can and can’t do)
- **Inviting questions**
- **Repeating the core idea in different ways**

And this matters because AI is already influencing how people write, work, learn, and even think. If only a small group can understand it, then only a small group gets to shape how it’s used.

Clear explanations are a form of empowerment. They help people make choices, set boundaries, and use technology with intention instead of anxiety.

A great “Explain It Like I’m Five” mindset is ultimately about respect: respect for the listener’s time, attention, and dignity.

## Closing Thought

If you can explain something simply, you don’t just prove you understand it—you make it shareable. And in a world full of noise, the most generous thing you can do is turn confusion into clarity, one human-sized sentence at a time.
