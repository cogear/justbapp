# Guilt, and getting over it

*Why comfort keeps getting interrupted — and how to let it stay.*

## The Tug

You finally sit down. Tea in hand, blanket over your knees, nothing that needs you for the next hour. The light is good. And then it arrives — that small tug in your chest that says you should be doing something else.

Not a thought, exactly. More like a hand on your shoulder. Guilt, showing up the moment comfort does, as if it had been invited.

If that's familiar, you're not broken. You're carrying something old. Almost everyone carries it. We learn early that stillness is something you earn, and that lesson sits deep in the body long after the mind has stopped believing it.

## A Borrowed Voice

Notice that nobody's in the room. No one is waiting on you, no one is keeping score, nothing is on fire. The tug isn't coming from outside. It's coming from somewhere behind your own eyes.

Guilt like this is a borrowed voice. Somewhere early on you learned that stillness had to be paid for — that you could sit down once the list was empty. The list has never once been empty. And you kept the lesson long after you forgot who handed it to you.

It's an old tape. It doesn't check the facts. It doesn't look around the room and see that you're alone, that no one is judging, that the moment is genuinely safe. It just plays.

The voice usually sounds like someone specific — a parent, a teacher, a boss you once had, a culture that praised you for staying busy. But by now, after enough years, it's just you. You're the one keeping it going. Which also means you're the one who can let it sit in the room without obeying it.

## No Address

So let's be plain about what guilt is. It's a feeling. Feelings carry information, and sometimes the information is simply out of date.

Real guilt has an address. You did something to someone, you know their name, and you can go make it right. That kind is useful. It points at a door.

This kind has no address. There's nobody to apologise to for sitting in your own chair. It's an alarm going off in an empty building.

Which is worth saying out loud: a feeling is not an instruction. You don't have to do what it tells you. You can feel the tug and stay exactly where you are.

A lot of us treat guilt as if it were a moral compass. But a compass only works if it's pointing at something real. This one is spinning. The guilt that arrives when you sit down isn't telling you anything true about the moment. It's telling you something true about your history — that you were taught to be useful before you were taught to be at ease.

## Stay Sitting

The practice is smaller than you want it to be. You don't argue with the guilt. You don't build a case for why you've earned the hour. You notice it, and you stay.

Say it plainly, in your head — that's just the guilt. Then keep sitting. Don't stand up. Don't go find a small task to pay it off with. Let the feeling be in the room, and let it lose interest in you.

Because it does lose interest. It's loud the first few times and quieter after that. Guilt is a habit like any other, and habits thin out when you stop feeding them. Every time you stay sitting, you teach the old tape something new: this room is safe, this quiet is allowed, no one is coming.

Some evenings it wins anyway. You'll be up wiping a counter that was already clean. That's fine. Notice, and sit back down.

The goal isn't to never feel the tug again. The goal is to stop treating it as authority. Once it's just a feeling in the room — one thing among many — it stops running the evening.

## Permission

You don't have to feel good about resting for the rest to do its work. The warmth reaches you whether or not you approve of it. Your body doesn't need your mind's permission to begin settling. It just needs you to stop leaving.

And after enough of these evenings, something quietly changes. You sit down, and nothing tugs. The quiet is just quiet. You're allowed to be in it.

You are allowed to rest before the work is finished. The work is never finished. That was never the deal.

The deal was always this: you get to be here, in the warm chair, with the good light, for no reason at all.
