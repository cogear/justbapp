# Ten years of Tuesdays

*What happens when you stop asking whether it's worth it and just keep the evening.*

## Tuesday, again

Someone I know has spent ten years of Tuesday evenings at the same table, with the same tools, making the same small thing. Badly, at first. Then less badly.

She never got good enough to teach it. She never got anywhere with it, in the way people mean when they say that. She just kept the Tuesday.

Ten years. That's five hundred evenings, give or take. Nothing dramatic ever happened in any one of them.

I think about her often when I'm tempted to quit something. Not because she's inspiring in the usual way — she's not a poster for discipline or grit. She's a poster for something quieter. She stopped weighing it. And then it stayed.

## The story we expect

We're used to a different story. You start something, you struggle, and then there's a stretch of time that gets skipped over. At the end of it, you're transformed. Progress with a shape you can see and maybe photograph.

So we check. Six weeks in, we hold the work up to the light and ask whether it's going anywhere. And when it isn't, we quietly stop. We don't even notice we've decided. The supplies just end up in a closet.

The trouble isn't the checking. It's that a single Tuesday can't answer that question. One evening never looks like progress. It just looks like an evening. You sit down. You do the thing. You get up. There's nothing to hold up to the light.

This is the part we don't talk about: most practice is invisible while it's happening. The accumulation is real, but it's not something you can witness in real time. You can only witness it later, looking back, and even then it's hard to point to the moment anything changed.

## Not going anywhere

But what if the Tuesday isn't a step toward anything? Not a phase. Not a season. Just the way you spend that evening.

Ten years was never the plan. Ten years is what happens when you stop deciding each week whether it's worth it. The evening stops being a question. It just becomes what Tuesdays are.

Ten years is one Tuesday, kept.

And something does gather. Your hands learn things you couldn't put into words. A kind of knowledge that lives in your fingers, not your head. But that's a side effect. It arrives on its own, and it doesn't need you to be watching for it. In fact, watching for it might be the one thing that slows it down.

There's a patience that only exists when you're not in a hurry. You can't manufacture it by trying. It's what's left over when the striving goes quiet.

## How a Tuesday survives

So pick the smallest version. Not the ambitious hour — the one you'd still keep on a bad week. Twenty minutes at the kitchen table counts. It counts completely. A small thing done regularly is not a lesser version of a big thing done occasionally. It's a different thing entirely, and in many ways it's the sturdier one.

Keep the tools where you can see them. If setting up takes ten minutes, the Tuesday will lose to the sofa. Make it take one. Friction is the quiet killer of practice. Not laziness — just the small accumulated weight of needing to find the scissors.

And let yourself miss some. A Tuesday you skipped isn't a broken streak. It's a Tuesday you skipped. The next one is still there, and it isn't holding it against you. This is important, so it bears repeating: guilt is not what keeps a practice alive. The practice keeps it alive. Guilt just makes it heavier until you can't pick it up.

## Permission

Ten years from now, you might have a drawer of imperfect things and no story to tell about them. No before and after. No arc. Nothing you'd post.

But you'll know what your hands do on a Tuesday. That's a steadiness you can't get any faster, and nothing else quite gives it to you.

You don't have to be going anywhere to keep going.
