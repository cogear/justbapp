# Reading your work, fixing your mistakes

*The fabric keeps a record of everything — and it's all still just string.*

## The Fabric Tells You

Somewhere in the first few inches, something will go wrong. A stitch slips. A row comes out crooked. You feel it in your hands before you see it — a slight pull, a place where the rhythm stuttered.

Then you hold the fabric up to the light, and it tells you exactly what happened, and where.

Knitting keeps a record of itself. Every move you made is looped into the thing you're holding. It's honest in a way that takes getting used to. There's no hiding a moment of distraction. The yarn simply received it and moved on.

## The Shelf

Most people stop right here. Not because knitting is hard, but because something looks wrong and they can't tell which part is wrong, or how far back it goes.

You count the stitches and get a different number than yesterday. You hold it up and something in the middle looks off, and the off-ness could be anywhere.

So the needles get set down on a shelf with the yarn still attached. A season passes. A year. I have done this. More than once.

Here's what I want to tell you: it isn't the mistake that ends things. It's not knowing that a mistake is allowed to be fixed.

We carry this idea that making something means getting it right, that a wrong move means the whole attempt is tainted. Knitting gently dismantles that. There is nothing here you cannot undo. Nothing is sealed. Nothing is permanent.

## It's One Piece of String

Your knitting is one long piece of string, looped through itself. That's all it is. Nothing is glued. Nothing is welded shut. Nothing you have done is final.

So learn to read it.

On the smooth side, every stitch makes a small V, and the V's stack into tidy columns. Run your finger up one column. You'll find where it breaks — a gap where a loop let go, a bump where a stitch turned the wrong way, a column that quietly became two.

The other side is a field of soft bumps, and it reads just as plainly. Same information, different handwriting. Give it a minute. Let your eyes adjust.

The fabric doesn't hide anything from you. It just waits for you to look.

This is one of the quiet gifts of working with your hands. You develop a kind of literacy. Not the kind measured in exams, but the kind your fingertips pick up. You start to notice things. A tension change. A rhythm shift. The work teaches you to pay a particular kind of attention, and that attention slowly becomes its own reward.

## Backwards Is Also Knitting

The fix is slower than the mistake was. That's the whole thing.

Slide the needle out of the last stitch you made. Pull the yarn gently. One stitch back. Then another. Your hands will do in reverse what they already learned to do forward. Same rhythm. Same quiet. Undoing is knitting, only pointed the other direction.

If the trouble is ten rows down, you have two choices, and both of them are fine. Go back and get it. Or carry on and let it live there.

Some fixes want to happen tomorrow instead. Put it down. It will wait. Yarn is patient in a way almost nothing else is.

There's something worth noticing in that patience. The work doesn't demand you finish it on any particular timeline. It sits in a bag or a basket, holding its shape, ready when you are. Very little in life is like that.

## A Place Where I Was Learning

I have a scarf with a twisted stitch in the eleventh row. I know exactly where it is. Nobody else has ever found it.

It isn't a flaw I'm forgiving. It's a place where I was learning something. The fabric held it and kept going, and so did I.

That scarf is one of my favorite things I've made, not despite the mistake but because of it. Every time I see that little twist, I remember what my hands were figuring out. The yarn remembers too, in its own quiet way.

Read your work. Fix what you want to fix. Leave what you want to leave. Nothing here is permanent, and nothing here is being graded. It's string, and it's yours.
