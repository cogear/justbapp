# Hooks, yarn, and the slip knot

*A hook, some yarn, and one knot you'll undo forty times. That's the whole beginning.*

## The whole tool

Somewhere in a drawer, or a shop, or a friend's spare basket, there's a small stick with a notch cut into one end. That's the whole tool. That's all crochet asks you to hold.

The design hasn't changed much in a very long time. There's something steadying about that. People have been doing this for centuries with basically the same implement in their hands — sitting in chairs, on porches, in doorways. You're joining a long, quiet line of people who just needed something to do with their hands.

One hand, one hook, one string. Nothing plugs in. Nothing needs a table or a setup or a clean surface. You can do this in a chair by a window, or on a bus, or in a waiting room with the television going. The portability is part of the gift. It goes where you go without asking much of the space.

## Somewhere in the middle

Hooks come in sizes. Numbers and letters stamped into the handle, a system that changes depending on the country and doesn't much matter yet. Somewhere in the middle is fine.

If you want a number: four millimetres. Or five. Aluminium, plastic, wood — whatever your hand meets without complaint. Some have thick soft grips. Some are thin and cold. You'll develop opinions later, once your hand knows what it likes.

Buy the cheap one. You are not investing in anything. This isn't a commitment ceremony. If it turns out you love this, you'll know exactly what you want by then — and that's a better way to spend money anyway, spending it on a preference you've earned rather than a guess.

## Not the beautiful one

Yarn is where people get lost. Walls of colour, fibre names, weight categories, a whole vocabulary printed on little paper bands. You can learn all that later. Or never.

What you want right now is smooth, medium weight, and light in colour. Pale grey, cream, soft blue — anything where you can see the shape of your own stitches. Dark yarn hides your work from you. Fuzzy yarn hides it too. You're learning to see what your hands are doing, and you need light for that.

And not the beautiful one. Not the hand-dyed skein you've been saving for something worthy. That one comes later, if it comes at all. Right now you want yarn you're willing to pull apart forty times. Yarn you can ruin without flinching.

## One knot

Everything in crochet starts the same way. One loop on the hook, held there by a knot that tightens or loosens when you pull the right end. The slip knot.

Lay the yarn across your palm. Cross the tail over the working strand to make a loop. Reach through, catch the strand behind it, and pull it up into a second loop. Slide the hook in. Then pull the tail, gently, until it sits close on the hook. Not tight. Snug.

It will feel like nonsense the first three times. Your fingers will fumble. The loop will be too big, then too small. You'll pull the wrong end and it'll vanish entirely.

Then, somewhere around the tenth or twentieth time, your hands start doing it without you — in the dark, while you're talking about something else entirely, while your mind is three rooms away.

That's the real promise here. Not that it's easy. That it becomes yours. That your body learns a thing your thinking mind doesn't have to supervise.

Undo it. Do it again. Nothing is wasted. The yarn doesn't remember being tied.

## Permission

That's today. A hook you can afford to lose, yarn you can afford to ruin, and one knot practised until it's boring.

You haven't made anything. You weren't supposed to. A loop on a hook isn't a thing — it's just your hands and this tool getting acquainted, learning how to be together.

Sit with the loop a while. There's nothing to finish, nothing to show. Just your fingers and this small notched stick and the quiet that comes when you let a simple movement repeat until it settles into you.

A good place to start.
