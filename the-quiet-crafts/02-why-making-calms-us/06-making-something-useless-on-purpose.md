# Making something useless on purpose

*A friend asked what I was making. The honest answer was nothing — and that's the whole point.*

## The kitchen table

A friend came by one evening and found me at the kitchen table with wire and a pair of pliers. She asked what I was making. I said, honestly, nothing.

Not nothing — there was a thing in my hands. A small wire shape, bending and curling into itself. It just wasn't for anything.

It took me a long time to say that out loud without adding an excuse afterwards. Some little justification. Some plan for it. "Just fiddling," I'd say, with a shrug that asked to be forgiven for the time.

I've come to think that's the whole point. The not-for-anything.

## What's it for?

Almost everything we make is expected to earn its place. A meal gets eaten. A shelf holds books. Even the things we do for pleasure get asked, sooner or later, what they're for. A scarf keeps someone warm. A drawing gets framed.

And there's a sentence that arrives, always kindly meant, usually from someone who loves you. You could sell those. You should make a set. Have you thought about a little shop?

It's a compliment. It's also a small door quietly closing.

Because now the thing has a job. And anything with a job can fail at it. Now there's a standard in the room with you — one that wasn't there before, when it was just you and the wire and the evening.

## Nothing to fail at

Think about what happens when an object is supposed to work. Does the lid fit. Does the joint hold. Is it straight enough to give away. Is it good enough to charge for.

You're not making anymore. You're inspecting. Your hands are doing one thing and your head is somewhere out ahead of them, already checking, already measuring against a version that doesn't exist yet.

The body knows how to do the work. The hands know. But the mind jumps ahead, and the quiet fills up with evaluation.

A useless thing can't be inspected. There was nothing it was meant to do, so there's no way for it to be wrong. It's just the shape your hands made this week.

No one is grading it. Not even you. And when the grading stops, something in you quietly stops standing guard. The shoulders drop. The breath finds its own pace.

## On purpose

So make the bowl too small to hold anything. Knit the square that will never become a blanket. Carve the spoon, and then don't cook with it. Keep it in a drawer.

Whittle a stick down to nothing and sweep the shavings into the bin. That counts. That was the whole of it, start to finish. The shavings were the evening.

Put it on the windowsill if you like looking at it. Or don't. It doesn't have to be displayed to have been worth the time.

## The permission underneath

The uselessness isn't a flaw you're putting up with. It's the reason the hour felt so quiet. Nothing was riding on it.

You weren't producing anything. You weren't rehearsing for some better version later. You were a person with something in their hands on an ordinary Tuesday evening.

That's enough. That was always enough.

The wire doesn't know it's useless. The wood doesn't know. Only we carry that word, and we can set it down whenever we're ready.
