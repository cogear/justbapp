# Your Body Knows

## The exhale you didn’t realize you were waiting for

There’s a particular kind of sigh that happens when you finally let yourself be comfortable.

Not the polite exhale you do when you sit down. Not the dramatic one you do when you’re annoyed. This one is deeper—like it comes from below your chest, from somewhere that’s been bracing for longer than you realized.

It’s the sound of your system standing down.

Sometimes we don’t notice how tense we’ve been until the moment we aren’t anymore. The exhale becomes evidence: *Oh. I was holding something.*

## The body’s quiet, constant intelligence

Your body is always running a kind of background check.

It knows you’re tired before you admit it. It knows you’re overwhelmed before you have the words. It knows when something feels safe, when something feels off, when you’re forcing yourself through a moment you’re not really okay with.

The mind is great at stories—reasons, explanations, justifications. The body is great at signals.

A tight jaw. A clenched stomach. Shallow breathing. Restlessness. A heavy fatigue that isn’t fixed by one more coffee. These are not character flaws. They’re information.

And yet many of us have been trained to override that information.

We push through tired because we equate rest with falling behind. We ignore tension because it’s “normal.” We treat the body like a machine to optimize—fuel it, upgrade it, keep it productive—rather than a wise companion trying to keep us well.

## The modern habit of disconnecting

Living with AI makes this even easier to do.

When answers are instant and productivity is always one tool away, it’s tempting to live from the neck up. We outsource memory, planning, even emotional processing. We refine prompts. We optimize routines. We track data.

None of that is bad. But there’s a subtle risk: we can start trusting systems more than sensations.

Your calendar might say you have time for one more meeting. Your body might say you’re done.

Your notes app might say you slept seven hours. Your nervous system might say it wasn’t restful.

The point isn’t to reject technology. It’s to remember that no external tool—no matter how advanced—can replace your internal signals. Your body is not “less accurate” because it’s not formatted like a dashboard.

## A quick body scan you can do in under a minute

Right now, without trying to fix anything, just notice your body.

Where are you holding tension?

Check your shoulders. Are they lifted like they’re trying to help you think?

Check your jaw. Are your teeth touching?

Check your forehead. Is it braced, like you’re concentrating on a problem you’re not currently solving?

Now, just for this moment, let your shoulders drop. Let your jaw unclench. Let your forehead smooth.

Notice what happens.

That small release? It matters. Not because it solves your life, but because it reveals something: you were carrying tension without realizing it. Your body had been communicating, and you finally turned the volume up enough to hear it.

This is how listening begins—simple noticing, repeated often.

## Comfort is physical, not just conceptual

Comfort isn’t only an idea. It’s an experience in the nervous system.

When you touch something soft, your body responds. When you feel warmth, something settles. When you sit somewhere supportive, your breathing changes. When you’re with someone safe, your shoulders soften without permission from your mind.

Sometimes comfort arrives so clearly it feels like a yes you can’t argue with.

A hot shower. A warm drink held between your palms. Sunlight on your skin. A blanket that’s heavier than it looks. A slow walk where nobody expects anything from you.

I think about sinking into a hot tub and feeling my whole body say yes. The heat reaches your bones. Your mind goes quieter—not because you forced it to, but because your system finally gets what it needs. In those moments, comfort isn’t something you *decide*. It’s something your body recognizes immediately.

## Permission is the practice

For many people, the hardest part of listening to the body isn’t understanding the signal.

It’s believing you’re allowed to respond to it.

We wait until we’ve “earned” rest. We wait until we’ve completed enough tasks to justify a pause. We wait until we’re sick, snappy, or burned out enough that stopping isn’t a choice anymore.

But your body doesn’t ask for permission in perfect sentences. It asks in sensations.

And the exhale—the real one—is often its way of saying: *Finally.*

## Trust the exhale

You don’t have to turn your life upside down to start listening.

Start small. Notice tension. Release it when you can. Let comfort count as useful data, not indulgence. Treat fatigue as information, not weakness. Ask yourself, gently, “What would help my body feel 5% more supported right now?”

Your body has been trying to tell you all along.

The practice is learning to believe it.

And when that deep, quiet exhale arrives—when your system softens and something in you lets go—pause for a moment. Let it be enough to know: you’re coming back to yourself.
