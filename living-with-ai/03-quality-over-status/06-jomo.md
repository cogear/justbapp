# JOMO: The Joy of Missing Out

## The Flip: From FOMO to Freedom

You’ve heard of FOMO—the fear of missing out. It’s the restless feeling that something better is happening somewhere else. It’s the anxious yes to plans you don’t want, the reflexive scroll through other people’s highlight reels, the quiet worry that you’re falling behind.

FOMO isn’t just about events. It’s about identity. The fear that if you’re not seen, included, invited, or participating, you’re somehow less relevant—or less alive.

But there’s another feeling that doesn’t get nearly as much attention.

JOMO: the joy of missing out.

Not a smug kind of “I’m above it all.” More like a calm exhale. The relief of realizing you don’t actually want what you thought you were supposed to want.

## The Joy: Relief Is Data

JOMO is that moment when everyone is going out, and you’re staying in—and you feel genuinely relieved. Not because you’re avoiding life, but because you’re choosing the version of life that fits you today.

It’s when you decline an invitation and your body softens instead of tensing up. When your mind gets quieter. When you don’t need an elaborate excuse, because you’re not trying to convince anyone.

That relief is information.

We tend to treat our preferences as negotiable and other people’s expectations as fixed. JOMO flips that. It says: *my peace matters enough to protect.*

And the truth is, missing something isn’t automatically a loss. Sometimes it’s a trade: you skip one thing to gain something else—sleep, time, closeness, focus, recovery, or simply the space to think.

## JOMO in Action: Choosing What You Actually Want

JOMO looks ordinary from the outside, but it changes everything on the inside.

It’s turning down a networking event because you’d rather have dinner with your family. Missing the concert because you need sleep more than you need a story for Monday morning. Skipping the trip because you’re craving a quiet weekend at home.

JOMO isn’t isolation. It’s alignment.

Because here’s the uncomfortable secret: a lot of the things we attend, we attend out of obligation. We show up physically, but mentally we’re elsewhere—checking our phone, watching the clock, feeling depleted. We went to avoid regret, but we didn’t even enjoy it.

That’s not living. That’s performing.

JOMO is simply honesty about what you actually want.

And in a world that rewards busyness and visibility, honesty can feel rebellious.

## Living With AI: Why JOMO Matters More Now

If you’re living in a world shaped by algorithms—recommendations, invites, curated feeds, constant updates—FOMO is practically engineered. Platforms thrive on the feeling that you might miss something important.

More content. More events. More “shoulds.”

AI makes it even easier to stay stimulated, connected, and informed. But “more” isn’t always better—it’s often just louder.

JOMO is one of the most human skills you can practice in an AI-saturated world: the ability to choose intentionally instead of reactively.

To pause before you click, commit, or compare.

To remember that your attention is not an unlimited resource.

## Cultivating JOMO: A Simple Practice That Works

The practice is simple, but not always easy:

When an invitation comes—pause.

Before you answer, ask yourself two questions:

1. **What do I actually want?**  
2. **What will this cost me?** (Energy, time, sleep, presence, recovery, focus)

The key is to stop asking what you *should* do. “Should” often belongs to someone else—your friend group, your workplace, your past self, or the person you’re trying to be.

Then honor the honest answer.

That might mean saying yes with enthusiasm. Or it might mean saying no without apology.

A helpful mindset shift: a clear calendar isn’t empty—it’s available.

Available for what energizes you. Available for rest. Available for creativity. Available for the relationships that feel real. Available for your own thoughts.

If you struggle with guilt, try replacing explanations with simple truth:

- “Thanks for thinking of me—I’m going to pass this time.”  
- “I’m staying in tonight, but I hope you have a great time.”  
- “I can’t make it, but I’d love to catch up another day.”

No drama. No over-justifying. Just clarity.

## Permission: Choose Your Missing

Missing out isn’t always loss. Sometimes it’s the wisest choice you’ll make all week.

JOMO gives you permission to choose your missing—to skip what drains you so you can keep what sustains you.

And the more you practice it, the more you learn something quietly powerful: you don’t need to attend everything to belong. You don’t need to keep up with everything to be enough.

Sometimes the best thing you can do is close the app, decline the invite, stay home, and feel your life returning to you—one intentional choice at a time.

*Closing thought: The goal isn’t to miss more. The goal is to miss what isn’t yours—and find joy in what you gain when you do.*
