# This Is What Is

## Four Words That Change the Weather Inside You

Four words changed everything for me:

**This is what is.**

They sound almost too simple to matter. Like something you’d mutter while shrugging at a missed train or a spilled coffee.

But used intentionally, they’re not a shrug. They’re a flashlight.

Not defeat. Not giving up. Not “welp, guess my life is ruined.”

More like the clearest possible seeing—an honest acknowledgment of what’s happening right now, without adding extra stories on top of it.

And that kind of clarity is surprisingly powerful.

## Why We Argue With Reality (Even When It Never Works)

When something goes wrong, most of us don’t just experience the event.

We also launch an internal legal case.

We defend ourselves. We build arguments. We rehearse the conversation we should have had. We come up with the perfect comeback after it’s too late to say it.

We rewrite the past, draft alternate endings, and then get emotionally attached to those imaginary versions as if they’re evidence.

It makes sense. The brain hates uncertainty and loss of control. “Arguing with reality” can feel like problem-solving. It can feel productive.

But it’s usually not.

Because you can’t win a fight against what already happened.

You can’t reason your way into a different email being sent, a different tone being used, a different outcome being chosen.

You can only burn energy insisting that life should have followed the script in your head.

## The Real Cost of Pretending Things Are Different

Here’s the sneaky part: the most exhausting situations aren’t always the hardest ones.

They’re the ones where we keep saying, internally:

- This shouldn’t be happening.  
- They shouldn’t be like this.  
- I shouldn’t have done that.  
- I should be further ahead by now.  

And each “should” is another tug-of-war rope you pick up.

The situation may be painful. But the extra suffering often comes from the refusal to let reality be real.

When you can’t accept the current moment, you can’t really work with it either. You’re stuck fighting shadows.

## The Pause: A Tiny Gap That Changes Everything

“This is what is” interrupts the fight.

It creates a pause—a small, quiet gap between what happened and what you do next.

In that gap, you get something many people rarely experience in the moment: **choice**.

Not the dramatic kind of choice where everything suddenly becomes easy.

More like the simple choice to stop spiraling for a second.

To stop feeding the story.

To stop adding a catastrophic future to a difficult present.

Importantly, this phrase does *not* mean:

- “This is fine.”
- “Everything happens for a reason.”
- “I’m not allowed to be upset.”

It’s not a positivity slogan. It’s not spiritual bypassing.

It’s a grounding statement. A way to say: **this is the truth of the moment.** The rest comes later.

## How to Use It in Daily Life (Without Forcing It)

I use these words daily—especially in the small moments that can quietly wreck a whole day.

When I catch myself catastrophizing. When I’m comparing my insides to everyone else’s outsides. When I feel behind. When the gap between who I am and who I think I should be feels unbearable.

“This is what is.”

Then, from that steadier ground, I can ask a much better question:

**What’s the next right thing?**

Not the next perfect thing. Not the thing that fixes your whole life in one move.

Just the next right thing.

Sometimes that looks like:

- Taking one breath before responding to a message  
- Admitting you made a mistake without trying to explain it away  
- Drinking water, eating something, going outside for five minutes  
- Asking for help instead of pretending you’re fine  
- Doing the smallest possible task to regain momentum  

Acceptance doesn’t remove responsibility. It removes distortion.

It helps you respond to the real situation—not the exaggerated version your mind is producing under stress.

## Acceptance Isn’t Approval—It’s Permission to Move Forward

This is the part people worry about.

If I accept reality, does that mean I’m approving of it?

No.

Acceptance means you stop arguing with what *is* so you can put your energy toward what *can be done*.

You are allowed to see clearly without judgment.

You are allowed to accept what is and still want more.

You are allowed to feel disappointed and still be steady.

You are allowed to say, “This hurts,” without turning it into, “This will ruin everything.”

“This is what is” is not a cage. It’s a door.

Because once you stop fighting the present, you regain access to your power in it.

## Closing Reflection

Try it the next time your mind starts building a case against reality.

Not to erase your feelings—just to anchor yourself in truth.

“This is what is.”

And then, gently: **What’s the next right thing?**
